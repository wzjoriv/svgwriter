Diagrams and Office files¶

Diagram languages come in through their own tools, and PowerPoint, Word and Excel files go both ways. Saving and the other formats are in Export and import.

Diagrams¶

Graphviz (.dot, .gv) and Mermaid (.mmd, .mermaid) files load through their own command-line tools, so layouts match what those tools draw: svg.Document.open("graph.dot") runs dot -Tsvg, and svg.Document.open("flow.mmd") runs mmdc with SVG (not HTML) labels. The result is an ordinary document to edit, combine or export to any format. Install Graphviz or @mermaid-js/mermaid-cli, or point SVGWRITER_DOT / SVGWRITER_MMDC at the executables; a missing tool raises DiagramToolError with the install hint. Without mmdc, Mermaid flowcharts (graph / flowchart: node shapes, edge styles and labels, chains, & groups, subgraphs) are parsed and laid out by svgwriter itself, so they need no tool at all.

PlantUML (.puml) and D2 (.d2) load the same way through plantuml and d2. Math comes from LaTeX itself: svg.latex(r"\int_0^1 x^2\,dx = \frac{1}{3}", display=True) returns the typeset formula as outlines. Without TeX it falls back to Ziamath (pure Python, pysvgwriter[math]) and then to matplotlib’s mathtext; engine="tex", "ziamath" or "mathtext" picks one.

Office files¶

pip install "pysvgwriter[office]" adds PowerPoint, Word and Excel:

  • .pptx out: one slide per drawing (a list gives a deck), each outline an editable PowerPoint shape (curves, solid or gradient fill, outline with dashes, caps and joins, transparency, hyperlink), text as editable text boxes, images as pictures and SMIL as slide effects. In: shapes, pictures, text boxes and effects of a slide (svg.Document.open("deck.pptx", slide=2)) as native SVG elements and presets, with the same paints and links.

  • .docx out: the drawing as native Word shapes floating in one paragraph as tall as the drawing, with the same outlines, paints, text boxes and pictures as the PowerPoint export. In: paragraphs, headings (Title, Heading 1-6), bulleted and numbered lists and line breaks laid out top to bottom like Markdown, with each run’s bold, italics, underline, strike-through, size, colour and font as a styled <tspan>. Inline pictures and shapes take their place in the flow; floating ones keep their position, and their text boxes come back as text. Table cells are read as paragraphs.

  • .xlsx out: the drawing as a picture at its natural size.

Formulas made with svg.latex remember their source (the group’s data-latex attribute), so the PowerPoint and Word exports write them as native, editable Office equations instead of outlines. The LaTeX goes through MathML (latex2mathml) to Office Math: fractions, scripts, roots, sums and integrals, accents, bars, limits, \left/\right delimiters, matrices, text and Greek letters. Each equation is centred on the box the formula occupied, in Cambria Math at the drawn size; in Word it floats over the shapes, or becomes an equation paragraph when the drawing holds nothing else. LaTeX with no Office equivalent stays as outlines. Reading a deck back gives an equation’s characters as plain text, without its layout.

import svgwriter as svg

slide = svg.Document(320, 120)
slide.add(svg.latex(r"\int_0^1 x^2\,dx = \frac{1}{3}", display=True)).scale(2).place(160, 60)
slide.save("formula.pptx")      # an editable equation, not shapes

Without the extra these formats raise an ImportError that names the install command.

Slide effects¶

PowerPoint keeps SMIL as slide effects that start with the slide, each at its own begin (no clicks needed), and reading a deck turns them back into element.animate presets:

SMIL (preset)

PowerPoint effect

opacity 0 to 1, 1 to 0 (fade_in, fade_out)

Fade entrance, Fade exit

set visibility (show, hide)

Appear, Disappear

translate or animateMotion (move_by, move_to, slide_in, follow, bounce, shake)

custom motion path

rotate (rotate, spin)

Spin

scale (scale, grow, shrink, pulse)

Grow/Shrink

fill or stroke colour (tween("fill", ...), two-colour color_cycle)

colour change of the fill or the line

begin, dur, repeat, freeze and there-and-back values (pulse, color_cycle) carry over; an ease becomes PowerPoint’s accelerate and decelerate fractions, so curves are approximated. Shapes with an entrance effect are drawn in their visible state, since PowerPoint hides them until the effect. PowerPoint spins and scales about the shape’s centre, so a named or custom pivot (about=, anchor) other than the centre is not kept, and motion paths are followed without turning. Anything else (morph, draw, create, write, wipe, typewriter, skew, blink, sync-based begin) is dropped with one warning that lists it.

Pictures where an effect has no shape¶

TikZ, EPS, PowerPoint, Word, Excalidraw and the polyline formats work from a flattened copy of the drawing: transforms, clipping, patterns and markers are worked into the shapes themselves. Masks and filters cannot be. PowerPoint and Word draw an element with a filter or mask as a picture of how it looks (one warning lists how many); everything else stays editable. save(..., fallback="drop") keeps those elements as shapes without the effect instead, and dpi= sets the pictures’ resolution (192 by default). The other flattened formats skip masks and filters with a warning; el.to_image(scale=...) turns any element into a picture by hand.

card = svg.Document(width=200, height=100)
card.rect(10, 10, 80, 50, rx=8, fill="teal", filter=svg.filters.drop_shadow())
# card.save("card.pptx")                   # the shadowed card becomes a picture
# card.save("card.pptx", fallback="drop")  # an editable card without its shadow
